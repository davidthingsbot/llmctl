{"one_positive_percent": 15.4, "two_positive_percent": 76.6, "independence_caveat": "The two-test figure assumes the two results are conditionally independent given true status, but repeating the same test on the same person usually is not: a false positive is often driven by a stable, person-specific cause (cross-reacting substance, an interfering antibody, a persistent biological quirk, sample or handling error, a miscalibrated instrument or lot). Those causes persist, so a person who tests falsely positive once is much more likely than average to test falsely positive again, and the errors are positively correlated. With correlated errors the second positive carries far less independent information than the calculation credits it with, so the true posterior after two positives is materially lower than 76.6 percent, and the number should be treated as an optimistic upper bound. A genuinely independent confirmation would require a different test with a different mechanism, ideally on a freshly drawn sample."}
